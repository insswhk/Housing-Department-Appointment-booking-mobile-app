## 1. Key numbers and rough savings estimate

From Housing Authority “Key Figures” as at 31 March 2023, there are:

- 193 public rental housing (PRH) estates in Hong Kong.[^1]
- 818,841 PRH units.[^1]
- 805,023 PRH households, average household size 2.63 persons.[^1]

Under the Well-off Tenants Policies, PRH households (after 10 years, or under certain tenancy policies) must declare income and assets every two years; forms are issued in April and October in batches, with some households exempt (all aged 60+, CSSA, disability allowance, etc.) .

If 805k households exist and declarations are biennial, a reasonable planning assumption is around 350k–450k households declaring each year after exemptions. This is not an official number but a working assumption for your business case.

### Rough savings

Using your scenario: if 100,000 households each lose 2–3 days, that is 200,000–300,000 man-days.
At a rough labour/productivity cost of HKD 500–1,000 per day, this is HKD 100M–300M equivalent value per year for 100k households.

Scaling to 300k–400k households affected:

- 300k households × 2 days × 500 = HKD 300M (low)
- 400k households × 3 days × 1,000 = HKD 1.2B (high)

So a realistic “order of magnitude” saving (public + government efficiency) can be framed as several hundred million HKD per year, plus non-quantified benefits: less leave taken, fewer queues, better data quality, fewer disputes, lower admin overhead, and higher satisfaction.[^1]

To build a serious proposal, you would phrase these as estimates and clearly state the assumptions.

## 2. End‑to‑end workflow (current vs proposed)

### Current manual workflow (simplified)

1. HA issues declaration forms in April/October cycles .
2. Tenant receives form, fills manually (income, assets, property ownership, etc.) .
3. Tenant visits estate office to make appointment (first leave day).
4. Officer gives date 1–2 days later.
5. Tenant returns with forms (second leave day).
6. Officer checks; if errors/missing documents, tenant corrects and comes again (third leave day).
7. Data is eventually keyed into HA systems, with manual verification and filing.

### Proposed semi-digital workflow

Keep in mind this is a government environment, with data-protection, audit, and inclusion for low-digital-literacy tenants.

1. **Tenant registration and authentication**
    - Tenant signs up in mobile/web app using HKID + PRH tenancy number + date of birth.
    - Backend validates against Housing Authority tenancy database (via secure API).
    - 2FA via SMS or app OTP.
2. **Digital appointment booking**
    - Tenant sees available time slots for their estate office for the declaration period.
    - Tenant books or reschedules appointments.
    - Estate office gets a dashboard showing all upcoming appointments.
3. **Digital pre‑submission**
    - Tenant downloads pre-filled e-form (with name, flat, HKID, previous cycle data).
    - Tenant fills missing details in app/web OR prints and fills by hand and uploads photo.
    - App allows upload of supporting documents (salary proof, bank statements, etc.) by photo or PDF.
    - System does basic validation (required fields, numeric ranges, HKID format, etc.).
4. **Back-office pre‑check**
    - Estate staff log into an internal portal.
    - For each appointment, they see form data and images of documents.
    - They can mark:
        - “Complete – ready for signing visit”
        - “Incomplete – please upload missing X, Y”
    - System sends push/SMS/email notifications to tenant indicating issues.
5. **On-site finalisation**
    - Tenant comes once to estate office, with missing originals.
    - Officer verifies identity, checks documents, finalises declaration.
    - Tenant signs digitally (tablet signature) or on paper that is scanned and linked.
    - System locks record, timestamps, and adds audit trail.
6. **Archiving and integration**
    - Data is stored in databases on Hong Kong servers, backed up per HA policies.
    - Summary data is pushed into existing HA systems for rent revision / tenancy actions .
    - All actions are logged: who checked, when, what was changed.

This design reduces visits from 2–3 to 1, and can fully support completely digital for a subset of tenants who are confident with e-signatures (depending on HK legal policy).

## 3. Functional requirements (what the system must do)

Structure this as if you are preparing a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for a government RFP.

### 3.1 Actors / user roles

- Tenant (PRH household representative).
- Estate Office Staff (frontline processing).
- Estate Manager / District Manager.
- Housing Authority Back‑office (policy, audit, IT).
- System Administrator (user management, configuration).


### 3.2 Tenant-facing functions

1. **Register and login**
    - Sign up with tenancy number + HKID + DOB + mobile/email.
    - 2FA login (SMS/OTP).
    - Password reset / account recovery.
2. **View tenancy and declaration status**
    - Show tenancy address, estate, next declaration cycle.
    - Show current status: Not started / In progress / Under review / Appointment booked / Completed.
3. **Appointment booking**
    - Check available appointment slots by date/time for the tenant’s estate office.
    - Book, cancel, reschedule subject to rules (e.g., cannot cancel within 24 hours).
    - Get confirmation (SMS, email, in-app).
4. **Form filling**
    - Online form for:
        - Income details (employment, self-employment, pensions, investments, etc.) .
        - Assets (cash, deposits, investments, properties, vehicles, etc.) .
        - Domestic property ownership declaration .
    - Auto-save drafts.
    - Pre-fill from last cycle where allowed.
5. **Document upload**
    - Take photo from mobile and upload (with on-device compression).
    - Multi-page PDF uploads.
    - Tag document type (salary proof, bank statement, etc.).
    - View uploaded documents history.
6. **Notifications**
    - In-app plus SMS/email for:
        - Forms issued (start of cycle).
        - Missing information / documents.
        - Appointment confirmation and reminders.
        - Declaration accepted / rejected.
7. **Accessibility**
    - Chinese (Trad), English; large fonts; high-contrast mode.
    - Simple, guided steps for elderly / low literacy.

### 3.3 Staff-facing functions

1. **Secure login (internal network / VPN)**
    - Use government SSO if available.
    - Role-based access to estates and districts.
2. **Appointment management**
    - View calendar of appointments per day, by counter/staff.
    - Re-assign appointments between staff.
    - Mark no-shows, completed, or rescheduled.
3. **Pre‑check queue**
    - List of pending cases with uploaded forms/documents.
    - Quick view of key figures: household size, declared income, assets.
    - Add comments and mark “complete” or “incomplete”.
4. **Case processing**
    - Open case details: forms, uploads, history.
    - Edit/confirm values during final visit (with change history).
    - Generate summary sheet for tenant to sign.
    - Flag suspected abuse / fraud for further investigation .
5. **Reporting and dashboards**
    - KPIs: number of forms received, completion rate, average processing time, number of corrections, etc.
    - Filters by estate, district, cycle.

### 3.4 Administrative \& integration functions

- Manage system configuration (cycles, templates, text).
- Manage user roles and permissions.
- Data export / integration with HA core systems (e.g., rent calculation, tenancy management) via API or batch files.
- Audit logging and reporting.

## 4. Non‑functional and government-specific requirements

### 4.1 Hosting and data residency

- All personal data must be stored on servers located in Hong Kong, under HK jurisdiction (on-premises or HK-based cloud, depending on HA policy).
- Use data centers certified to ISO 27001 or equivalent.
- Data retention and deletion policies must align with HA guidelines and Hong Kong’s Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance.


### 4.2 Security and privacy

- End-to-end HTTPS/TLS with HSTS.
- At-rest encryption for database and file storage.
- Role-based access control; least privilege.
- Detailed audit logs of every login, view, change, and export.
- Regular penetration testing and vulnerability scanning.
- Compliance with HA and HKSAR Government IT security standards.


### 4.3 Availability and performance

- High availability design (load balancers, redundant instances).
- RTO/RPO defined and agreed (e.g., RTO 4 hours, RPO 1 hour).
- Ability to handle peak loads during declaration windows (April/October) .


### 4.4 Usability and inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility guidelines where possible.
- Bilingual UX; minimal text; strong iconography.
- Offline-friendly design where possible (e.g., allow draft completion offline and submit when online).


### 4.5 Legal/compliance considerations

- Explicit consent and privacy notice in compliance with PDPO.
- Clear instructions about legal consequences of false declaration, consistent with existing WTP documentation .
- Logically equivalent to paper process to be legally valid; consult Department of Justice on digital signatures and evidence.

## 5. AI-assisted development workflow (for you using Cursor / similar tools)

Given your .NET and SQL background, a realistic stack:

- Backend: ASP.NET Core Web API.
- Frontend: React or Blazor (for web), plus a cross-platform mobile app (MAUI, React Native, or Flutter).
- Database: SQL Server.
- Storage: object storage for document images (on HK servers).


### 5.1 High-level development steps

1. **Requirements and domain modelling**
    - Convert the above functional/non-functional requirements into a formal SRS document.
    - Model core entities: Tenant, Household, DeclarationCycle, DeclarationForm, IncomeItem, AssetItem, Document, Appointment, StaffUser, Estate, AuditLog.
2. **Architecture design**
    - Draw a simple component diagram:
        - Mobile/Web Client
        - API Gateway
        - Application Services (Auth, Tenancy, Declarations, Appointments, Documents)
        - Database + File Storage
        - Admin \& Staff Portal
    - Decide integration method with existing HA systems: REST APIs vs batch.
3. **Database design**
    - Use SQL Server ERD:
        - Tables for Tenancy, HouseholdMember, DeclarationHeader, DeclarationIncome, DeclarationAsset, Document, Appointment, StaffUser, AuditLog.
    - Define indexes for queries by estate, cycle, date.
4. **Backend implementation (with AI help)**
    - In Cursor, define C\# models and DbContext for EF Core; ask AI to scaffold controllers for CRUD around Declarations and Appointments.
    - Implement authentication and tenancy verification.
    - Implement business logic: allowed edit windows, state transitions, validation rules per household type (e.g., exemptions) .
5. **Frontend and mobile**
    - Build a web app first (simpler for pilot) with login, dashboard, forms, upload.
    - Then build a mobile app using shared APIs; use AI assistant to generate basic React Native/Maui pages and then refine.
    - Focus on wizard-style UI for tenants.
6. **Staff portal**
    - Build a role-based web app: appointment calendar, case list, case detail view, reports.
    - AI tools can scaffold tables, filters, and forms quickly from your models.
7. **Document handling**
    - Use a service for image compression and optional OCR (to speed up staff checking).
    - Store files with secure URLs and access control.
8. **Testing and security**
    - Unit tests for core business rules (who must declare, biennial cycles, exemption logic).
    - Integration tests for full declaration flow.
    - Security review and pen test.
9. **Pilot and rollout**
    - Run a pilot in 1–2 estates with mixed demographics.
    - Collect metrics: reduction in visits, common errors, user feedback.
    - Refine UX; then expand gradually.

### 5.2 How to “drive” AI tools like Cursor for this

For each major piece, prompt AI with very concrete instructions:

- “Generate EF Core entities and DbContext for these tables: …”
- “Create ASP.NET Core minimal API endpoints for Appointment management with these rules: …”
- “Write React page for tenant declaration wizard with steps: household, income, assets, upload documents, review.”

You then review, refactor, and enforce security and standards. AI becomes your code generator and copilot, but you own architecture and compliance.

## 6. Business case narrative you can pitch

Key points you can include in a proposal document:

- There are 193 PRH estates and about 805k PRH households in Hong Kong.[^1]
- A large portion of these households are subject to biennial income and asset declarations under the Well-off Tenants Policies .
- The current paper-based process causes multiple estate-office visits, heavy manual checking, and repeated corrections, especially for low-income households with limited flexibility in working hours.
- A digital pre-submission and appointment system can:
    - Reduce tenant visits from 2–3 days off work to 1 short visit.
    - Save hundreds of thousands of man-days annually.
    - Avoid indirect economic loss estimated in the hundreds of millions of HKD per year.
    - Improve data quality, reduce fraud, and provide better reporting to HA.
- The system fully respects government requirements: HK-based servers, strong security, bilingual UI, and inclusivity for elderly/low-tech tenants.

This is exactly the kind of “pain-point from the ground” that internal tech teams may not see clearly because they do not personally experience the declaration process.
